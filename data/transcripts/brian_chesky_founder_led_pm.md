[00:00:15] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Brian Chesky, co-founder and CEO of Airbnb. Brian, you recently caused a huge stir in the tech world by saying Airbnb got rid of traditional product management. What did you actually mean by that?

[00:01:10] Brian Chesky: Thanks Lenny. People completely misunderstood that headline. We didn't get rid of the product management function—we combined product marketing with product management, and we elevated product design to be equal partners. But more importantly, we got rid of the divisional, siloed PM structure where hundreds of PMs were acting like mini-CEOs running their own isolated roadmaps.

[00:03:45] Brian Chesky: In the traditional Silicon Valley model, a company scales by hiring general managers and PMs who run autonomous pods. The CEO only sees a quarterly review. What happens? The product becomes fragmented, features don't talk to each other, and you lose quality. When COVID hit and our business dropped 80% in eight weeks, I realized we had 10 divisions doing 10 different things, none of which felt like one unified Airbnb.

[00:07:20] Lenny Rachitsky: So how do you run product reviews now? What is the actual cadence of founder-led product management?

[00:08:05] Brian Chesky: We run on a single, shared roadmap across the entire company. There are only two major release cycles a year: our Summer Release and our Winter Release. Every single feature that ships goes through a bi-weekly or weekly review with me, Hiroki (Head of Design), and our VP of Engineering.

[00:10:30] Brian Chesky: People said, "Brian, that doesn't scale. You're micromanaging." But I call it being in the details. Steve Jobs was in the details. Walt Disney was in the details. Great design and great product cohesion require someone holding the unified vision. If the CEO doesn't know what is shipping on the homepage, nobody owns the aggregate user experience.

[00:14:22] Brian Chesky: I personally review every major product change weekly. We don't use PowerPoint slides in reviews. We look at real Figma mockups, interactive prototypes, and production builds. If you can't click through the user journey in five minutes, you haven't simplified it enough.

[00:18:32] Lenny Rachitsky: What advice do you give to founders or VP of Products who want to adopt this founder-led model without creating bottlenecks?

[00:19:15] Brian Chesky: First, reduce the number of projects. We cut our roadmap by 80%. We do 10 big things exceptionally well rather than 100 mediocre experiments. Second, hire product managers who understand product marketing. If a PM cannot write the launch press release, explain why customers care, and define the value proposition in one sentence, they are just managing Jira tickets. Third, elevate designers. Designers are not service bureaus that make buttons pretty; they are architects of the customer experience.
