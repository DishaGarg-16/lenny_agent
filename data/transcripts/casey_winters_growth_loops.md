[00:00:10] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Casey Winters, former Chief Product Officer at Eventbrite and Growth Lead at Pinterest and Grubhub. Casey, why do you argue that traditional growth funnels are fundamentally broken?

[00:01:15] Casey Winters: Thanks Lenny. Funnels are a linear abstraction: Acquisition -> Activation -> Retention -> Referral -> Revenue. The fatal flaw with funnels is that they treat growth as something you pour money into at the top. Once a cohort reaches the bottom, the value stops unless you pour more paid acquisition into the top.

[00:04:00] Casey Winters: The fastest-growing products—Pinterest, Airbnb, Figma, Grubhub—do not grow through linear funnels. They grow through closed Growth Loops. In a growth loop, the output of one cohort of users becomes the input for the next cohort of users.

[00:06:45] Casey Winters: Take Pinterest as a classic User-Generated Content (UGC) loop:
1. A user creates or saves a pin.
2. Search engines index that pin.
3. A new user searches Google, finds the pin, and signs up.
4. That new user saves more pins, which get indexed, driving more new users.
That is a compounding, sustainable engine that gets more efficient over time, rather than more expensive.

[00:11:30] Lenny Rachitsky: How should product teams think about user onboarding? What causes most onboarding drops?

[00:12:15] Casey Winters: Most onboarding flows fail because product teams try to teach the user how to use the interface instead of helping the user experience value. If your onboarding is a 7-step modal tutorial pointing at buttons, 80% of users will click 'Skip'.

[00:15:00] Casey Winters: Great onboarding is about getting the user to the core transaction in the minimum number of clicks. At Grubhub, the core transaction was ordering food. We didn't ask you to create an account, verify an email, or customize dietary preferences until *after* you selected a restaurant and added items to your cart. Delay friction until the user has established intent.

[00:18:40] Lenny Rachitsky: What is the single most important metric for evaluating product-market fit?

[00:19:20] Casey Winters: A flattening retention curve. If your cohort retention curve never flattens parallel to the x-axis, you do not have product-market fit, and no amount of growth hacking or paid ads will save the business. Once the curve flattens—even at 20% or 30%—you have a viable core to build compounding growth loops around.
