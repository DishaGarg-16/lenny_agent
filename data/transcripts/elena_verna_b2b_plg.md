[00:00:20] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today we have Elena Verna, who has advised companies like Miro, Amplitude, Dropbox, SurveyMonkey, and Netlify on Product-Led Growth (PLG). Elena, what is the biggest misconception teams have when transitioning from sales-led to product-led?

[00:01:45] Elena Verna: Lenny, the biggest trap is thinking that PLG is just a self-serve checkout page or a 14-day free trial. PLG is not a go-to-market tactic; it is an organizational operating model where the product itself acquires, activates, monetizes, and retains users.

[00:04:30] Elena Verna: There are three non-obvious laws of B2B PLG that I see teams get wrong all the time. 

[00:05:10] Elena Verna: Law #1: Monetize the value, not the onboarding. Most companies gate their most valuable activation features behind a paywall too early. In PLG, you want users to experience the "Aha!" moment and build habit loops before you ever ask for a credit card. At Miro, we let users create up to 3 editable boards with unlimited collaborators for free. That created an organic viral loop where free users invited their entire teams.

[00:09:40] Lenny Rachitsky: That brings up B2B viral loops. How do you distinguish between artificial virality and sustainable product loops?

[00:10:25] Elena Verna: Real PLG virality comes in two flavors: Inherent Virality and Collaboration Virality. Inherent virality is when using the product requires sending an artifact to someone else—like sending a Calendly link, an e-signature in DocuSign, or a survey in SurveyMonkey. Collaboration virality is when the product gets better as colleagues join—like Figma, Notion, or Slack. If your product does not have natural collaboration, forcing a "refer a friend" button will fail 99% of the time.

[00:14:50] Lenny Rachitsky: What about pricing? When should a B2B product use freemium versus a free trial or reverse trial?

[00:15:35] Elena Verna: If you have high top-of-funnel demand and low marginal cost per user, Freemium is king because it maximizes distribution. If your product requires extensive configuration or has high usage costs (like AI compute or complex data pipelines), a Reverse Trial is often best. A Reverse Trial gives the user full Enterprise tier access for 14 days; if they don't buy, they gracefully drop down to a persistent free tier rather than being locked out completely.

[00:19:10] Elena Verna: The third law of PLG is: Product Qualified Leads (PQLs) beat Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs). Sales teams shouldn't cold call people who downloaded a PDF eBook. Sales should reach out when a team of 10 users is actively using the product daily and hitting workspace limits. That is how you combine PLG with Enterprise Sales.
