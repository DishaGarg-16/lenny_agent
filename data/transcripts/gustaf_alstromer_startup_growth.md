[00:00:15] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Gustaf Alströmer, Group Partner at Y Combinator and formerly on the early Growth team at Airbnb. Gustaf, having evaluated thousands of YC startups, what is the #1 mistake founders make when thinking about growth?

[00:01:25] Gustaf Alströmer: The #1 mistake is trying to scale distribution before having retention. Founders launch on Product Hunt, buy Google ads, get 5,000 signups, and celebrate. But three months later, 98% of those users have churned. Growth is a leaky bucket problem. If you don't plug the leak (retention), more water at the top is completely wasted.

[00:05:10] Gustaf Alströmer: At YC, we look at cohort retention graphs every single week. For consumer apps, good 30-day retention is around 25-40%. For B2B SaaS, good annual net revenue retention (NRR) is 110%+. If your numbers are below that baseline, stop marketing and go talk to the 10 users who love your product to understand what is working for them.

[00:09:45] Lenny Rachitsky: What was the most important growth lesson from your time building growth at Airbnb?

[00:10:30] Gustaf Alströmer: At Airbnb, we realized that supply drove demand, not the other way around. In a two-sided marketplace, if you have high-quality, unique supply in a city (like Paris or San Francisco), travelers will find you organically. We spent 80% of our growth engineering effort on host onboarding, automated pricing tips, and professional photography for listings. Unlock the supply constraint, and demand follows naturally.

[00:15:10] Lenny Rachitsky: How should early-stage teams structure their growth experiments?

[00:15:50] Gustaf Alströmer: Focus on high-velocity, small-batch experiments. Don't build a 3-month feature without testing customer intent first. Put up a fake door button, send personal manual emails, or build a simple landing page. If 10 out of 20 customers won't pre-order or manually commit, building the automated version is a waste of engineering bandwidth.
