[00:00:10] Lenny Rachitsky: Welcome back to Lenny's Podcast. Today's guest is Shreyas Doshi, who led product teams at Stripe, Twitter, Google, and Yahoo. Shreyas, you are famous for your mental models. Let's start with the LNO Framework for time management and prioritization.

[00:01:20] Shreyas Doshi: Thanks Lenny. Most PMs are constantly overwhelmed because they treat every single task with equal perfectionism. The LNO framework categorizes work into three distinct buckets: Leverage, Neutral, and Overhead.

[00:03:15] Shreyas Doshi: 'L' is Leverage Tasks. These are the 10x impact activities—defining product strategy, crafting a breakthrough product requirement document (PRD), conducting deep customer insight interviews, or running a critical pre-mortem before launch. You should give Leverage tasks 100% of your energy, creative focus, and polish.

[00:06:40] Shreyas Doshi: 'N' is Neutral Tasks. These are essential, day-to-day responsibilities—answering Slack messages, running weekly sprint standups, writing status updates. For Neutral tasks, the goal is "good enough" (80% quality). Spending extra hours perfecting a status update delivers zero incremental value.

[00:09:10] Shreyas Doshi: 'O' is Overhead Tasks. These are low-leverage bureaucratic necessities—expense reports, compliance check-ins, routine admin. You should actively minimize, delegate, or execute Overhead tasks as fast as humanly possible (50% effort).

[00:13:00] Lenny Rachitsky: You also talk about the Pre-Mortem technique for product launches. How does a team run an effective pre-mortem?

[00:13:45] Shreyas Doshi: Before you write a single line of code or launch a feature, gather the cross-functional team (PM, Design, Engineering, Marketing) and say: "Imagine it is six months from today. The product launched, and it was a catastrophic failure. Write down exactly why it failed." 

[00:16:30] Shreyas Doshi: This psychological shift removes the social taboo of critiquing a project. People will instantly identify the hidden assumptions—like unscalable onboarding friction, lack of retention hooks, or uncompetitive pricing. You take the top three risks from the pre-mortem and design mitigations directly into your PRD.

[00:20:15] Shreyas Doshi: Finally, the trait that separates top 1% PMs from average PMs is High Agency. A high-agency person bends reality to find a way to achieve the goal, regardless of organizational constraints or lack of resources.
